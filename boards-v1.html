Our Models

Iconoclast board models are constantly evolving, tapping elements of the most dynamic designs from the relatively short history of surfing and blending those with the best materials to create a superior and unique surfing experience.

"If you find yourself stagnant and not innovating, you'll be left behind. We're constantly seeking to innovate and… 'Don't be a sheep' – that's our motto."

Small Wave

Designs optimized for everyday conditions

Fish - Classic Swallow Tail

The Fish began its ascent as a wide-short kneeboard along the jagged cliffs of Point Loma in San Diego, under the astute guidance of Steve Lis and Skip Fry. This efficient displacement hull evolved into a connective species for upright bi-pedal surfers. Range from the very short 5-5 zone to a "long fish."

Edge

George Greenough discovered that placing a narrower sharp planing hull inside a wider and softer outline creates lift and reduces drag. Edge boards go faster the steeper, hollower, and cleaner the wave, like no other design… EVER. Adapted to be user-friendly from fish to Mavericks guns.

Bonzer

Campbell Brothers Malcolm and Duncan pride themselves on dynamic bottom contours and complementary 3 and 5 fin designs. Tapping the Venturi Effect with radical deep concaves, spiral "Vee" and steeply canted front fin placement, these UFOs harness lift and speed for acute angled attack.

Bonzer-Edge

The 2023 Best in Show honored the Campbell Brothers at the Boardroom Show. We dropped the Bonzer inside of an edge board to create the Bonzer-edge, resembling the fluted concaves of a power boat planing hull. A surprisingly loose and fast sliding experience.

Shortboard

High-performance shortboards adapted for groms, local schralpers, and aspiring QS warriors. Highly maneuverable, mostly rockered-out concave wave tools, best for steep and hollow conditions. We collaborate with Grant "Twiggy" Baker on these modern designs.

Sting

Ben Aipa created the sting design in da islands to marry planing speed with a sharp pivot point on the rail about a third up from the tail. Our model has an updated modern bottom with double-concave blending into "spiral Vee" to accentuate the bump wing.

Hull

A proper Greg Liddle-style displacement hull is a curvaceous thing of beauty and the essence of small wave efficiency. Ranging from small 5-8 flying saucers to 11' gliders, typically paired with a flexible Volan fin, hard-wired to trim in the pocket.

Finless Edge

This model pushed the Greenough design to its logical extremity – deepening concaves, hardening edges, and removing fins. Won the Santa Cruz Boardroom Best in Show. The 8-0 and 6-0 versions were barreled and spun at Steamer Lane and Pipe. Not for the faint of heart.

Mid-Length

The Swiss Army Knife of surf vehicles

Sim-nough

Our featured midlength: half hull up front combined with an edge board in the rear (Simmons + Greenough). Incredible glide and paddle speed of a Bob Simmons hull with lift and dynamism of a Greenough edge. Typically 6-6 to 8-0. Surf down as groveler, up as mini-gun, or in between.

Mid-Length Versatility

Popular because they work in wide variety of conditions. For some, a long-term romance, not a recent fad. Incredibly versatile with various fin setups: twin, 2+1, single, Bonzers, and our new Tri-zer model. Not just for board room types with pot bellies.

Longboard

From high-performance to classic logging

High-Performance

A functional tool for heavier beach breaks or larger surf where a log won't dare. Rockered out, "Vee" bottomed model that reacts quickly in steeps and hollows but can still nose ride. Inspired by decades coaching local youth and competing in California Longboard Coalition events.

Edge Longboard

Another option for logging in heavier surf – the narrower center plane with round-pin tail makes for a dynamic whip. Paired with an 8.9" Greenough "PowerGlide" from True Ames. Has made the Coalition finals up and down the Cali coast. 8-0 to 9-5.

Moontail Noserider

Owes a debt to Hall of Fame Builder William "Blinky" Hubina. Blended concave in front, healthy volume middle, kick in tail. Frees this progressive log to both glide and pivot sharply. Propelled by a crescent-shaped 10" noseriding fin. Sweet spot 9-3 to 9-8.

Glider

An incredibly satisfying antidote to over-active surfing. Glide along with minimalistic style like a Hawaiian chief or Skip Fry, levitating past the peons. Surprisingly small fins relative to length. 10' to 11', also useful as commute vehicles, paddling spot to spot.

Classic Log

Beef end-to-end with teardrop concave under nose. Finds its home in small, smooth, point break surf – Waikiki, Cowells, San-O. Nose width around 19.5", wide point near 23", tail 17.5". Building blocks for drop-knee turns and nose riding. 9-8 to 10-2.

Pig

Small wave species with devotees who love the narrow nose and wide set-back hips for agility in tight spaces. Legend has it Dale Velzy accidentally put a fin on the nose of a board, rather than the tail, and it went great – the pig was born.

Big Wave

When conditions get serious

Step-Up

Any small wave model can be stretched for bigger waves. Relative to rider – if your go-to shortboard is 5-11, a step-up could range 6-2 to 6-6. Similar outline to shortboard but with added length, width, and volume to handle bigger waves.

Semi-Gun

Evolves into different animal: trim outline with wide-point forward, volume under chest for paddle power. Rounded pintail for tighter hold on steeper, hollower sections. 6-10 to 8-0 range. An edge version gives extra gear in bigger, hollower, faster conditions.

Gun

When conditions get really serious, you want custom equipment. Order out of season, well before winter. We work with 3-time World Champ Grant "Twiggy" Baker, Mavericks Video Award winner Wilem Banks, and Jaws aficionado Steve Rice. What we learn from our team applies directly to your model.

Tow Board

Twig's tow models based on Nazare' boards – around 5-10, 2" thick, inside 18" wide. Shaped with heavy 5/8" T-band stringers, black tow density foam, triple-6 oz. glass, and carbon-innegra weave. Special sauce on bottom contours ensures speed and control when conditions get spicy.

A Note on Materials

Pushing boundaries with eco-friendly materials is not only for the environment's sake but also for improved performance. Meeting Dave Rastavich in Australia in 2019, he showed us a flax fish by Gary McNeill he'd been surfing for over 5 years. Grant Newby connected us with key suppliers of alternative materials in Australia – Sanded and Colan, purveyors of flax, basalt, flax-basalt, carbon-innegra, hemp and other specialized woven fabrics. We've experimented with Arctic Foam bio-blanks, Polyola, and Grow Blanks agave blanks with Entropy bio-resin. Most recently, a collaboration with SwellCycle 3D printed blanks has entered testing, with filament made from recycled plastic hospital trays.
